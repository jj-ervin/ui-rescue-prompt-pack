# 03 — Visual Hierarchy & Content Clarity

**Use when:** Visitors cannot quickly tell what the website does or where to go.

## Copy-paste prompt

Act as a UI designer and information architect.

**Website or materials:** [INSERT]
**Audience:** [INSERT]
**Primary visitor action:** [INSERT]

Inspect homepage value proposition, headings, section order, content density, navigation labels, competing calls to action, and supporting-information placement.

Answer: What does the website appear to offer? Who appears to benefit? What is its most prominent action? What might confuse a new visitor? What information is buried? Identify whether these are direct observations or audience assumptions.

Apply a five-second clarity **heuristic**, not a claim of real user testing. Produce a proposed page outline with ordering, primary/secondary actions, and up to three improved heading or CTA examples that do not invent capabilities, proof, or promises.

Label recommendations as content rewrite, visual reordering, or functional navigation change. Preserve factual meaning and accessibility-relevant heading semantics. **Do not implement yet.**

## Working rules
- Examine only materials actually accessible; separate observed defects from suspected risks.
- Preserve existing functionality, routes, content meaning, brand identity and architecture unless explicitly authorized.
- Do not invent inspection, test results, evidence, testimonials, or accessibility compliance.
- If changes need code access, do not claim they were made without editing and verifying them.
