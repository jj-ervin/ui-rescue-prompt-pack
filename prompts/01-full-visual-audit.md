# 01 — Full Website Visual Audit

**Use when:** The site feels wrong and you need to identify the highest-value fixes.

## Copy-paste prompt

Act as a senior UI/UX designer and website usability reviewer. Critically evaluate my existing website and identify the most valuable design improvements.

**Website, screenshots or files:** [INSERT]
**Audience:** [INSERT or identify likely audience and label as inferred]
**Primary website goal:** [INSERT]

Start by listing which pages, screenshots, viewports, and source files you actually inspected and what you could not inspect.

Evaluate first impression and clarity; information hierarchy; typography; spacing and alignment; color and contrast; navigation and calls to action; mobile responsiveness; accessibility; repetitive AI-generated patterns; and overall visual coherence.

For every significant issue report: (1) location and directly observed evidence, (2) why it matters, (3) precise change recommended, (4) critical/important/optional priority, and (5) verification method. Do not manufacture problems to complete the checklist.

Finish with the **five highest-impact changes**, their dependencies, and recommended order. Distinguish cosmetic changes from those that could affect behavior. Output an audit, not speculative implementation results.

**Do not modify files. Stop for approval.**

## Working rules
- Examine only materials actually accessible; separate observed defects from suspected risks.
- Preserve existing functionality, routes, content meaning, brand identity and architecture unless explicitly authorized.
- Do not invent inspection, test results, evidence, testimonials, or accessibility compliance.
- If changes need code access, do not claim they were made without editing and verifying them.
